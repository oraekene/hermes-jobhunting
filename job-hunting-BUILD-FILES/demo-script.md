# Demo Script

Built from the 24 demonstration-marked flows, arranged as a narrative rather than in catalog order. Roughly 37 minutes of material; the first two acts are 13 minutes and are the ones that decide whether anyone watches the rest.

Each beat gives the situation to set up, what to say while it runs, and the line that lands it. Every scenario below is the same text the manual uses, because a demo that shows something the documentation does not describe is a demo that sells a product nobody receives.

## Act 1 — It knows who you are  ·  4 min

### First install, experienced candidate

**Set up** — a fresh install with nothing set up yet

**Show** — Session one, run to completion: pair the approval channel, then Phase 0-4 of the career interview, then the handful of settings the pipeline cannot run without. Everything else is spread across the following fortnight.

**The moment** — A story you tell in passing is read back to you before it is stored, so a detail you misremembered does not become the version used in every future application.

### Career pulse journal check-in

**Set up** — journal cadence

**Show** — Raw entries are stored immediately; anything that looks like a durable career fact goes through confirmation before it reaches memory. A quantified claim heard in a voice note is read back before it is written.

**The moment** — A story you tell in passing is read back to you before it is stored, so a detail you misremembered does not become the version used in every future application.

## Act 2 — The spine  ·  9 min

### Scheduled discovery to staged application

**Set up** — discovery every 3h, sweep on its own cadence

**Show** — The spine. A posting appears, is parsed, scored, researched, tailored, gated for evidence, and arrives as one message asking approve, edit or skip. Nothing between the posting appearing and that message needs you.

**The moment** — You approve each research pass. Applications wait on you.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *strict fidelity* — unsupported claims stripped, gap written to open_gaps, nothing held for review
- *balanced fidelity* — claims applied and tagged UNVERIFIED, held for review before the approval message

### Score a role without applying

**Set up** — how well do I match this

**Show** — Read-only. Produces a score and the specific gaps behind it, and stops. Often the honest answer to "should I bother with this one."

### Edit before approving

**Set up** — reply 'edit' to an approval message

**Show** — An edit re-enters the evidence gate rather than bypassing it, and the edit itself is logged — the learning loop needs to know which packages needed changing.

**The moment** — A Product Manager role at Acme is filled in and ready. You get a message with the company, the role, a screenshot of the completed form and the list of claims made. Nothing is sent until you reply approve. If you say nothing, it waits.

### Applying to a posting you found yourself

**Set up** — pasted URL or job description

**Show** — The same pipeline entered halfway. Skips discovery and the daily cap entirely — a posting you brought is never rationed.

**The moment** — You approve each research pass. Applications wait on you.

## Act 3 — Why you can trust the output  ·  6 min

### The evidence gate refuses a claim

**Set up** — a tactic with no supporting line in memory

**Show** — The gap goes to a worklist, never into memory as an invented fact. The memory interview picks it up on its next run, which is how a refused claim becomes a real story rather than a permanent blocker.

**The moment** — strict — every claim cites a specific line in your resume, portfolio or story bank. Where no evidence exists the tactic is dropped and the gap is flagged to you.

### Submit blocked by the safety hook

**Set up** — a submit-shaped click with no approval recorded

**Show** — The hook trusts the recorded decision, never the conversation. It fails closed on every branch it can see — and fails open, silently, on any tool name it was never told about, which is why the form is not opened at all until the toolset is confirmed watched.

**The moment** — A Product Manager role at Acme is filled in and ready. You get a message with the company, the role, a screenshot of the completed form and the list of claims made. Nothing is sent until you reply approve. If you say nothing, it waits.

### The posting disappeared before approval

**Set up** — re-verification before the approval message

**Show** — Three signals, and the third catches most real cases: a 404, a page that now says closed, and a redirect to the board index where the fetch succeeds and only the content tells you. A pulled posting is information, not a decision — and it must not count as a non-reply, or every response rate you measure is understated.

### Answering a long application form

**Set up** — posting carries free-text questions

**Show** — Answers are recomposed from confirmed stories. Where no story covers the question, the gap is surfaced rather than invented, and the question joins the bank for next time.

**The moment** — A story you tell in passing is read back to you before it is stored, so a detail you misremembered does not become the version used in every future application.

## Act 4 — Beyond applying  ·  8 min

### Reply to a hiring post found on social

**Set up** — social listening scan

**Show** — A post asking for candidates becomes a drafted response with the post it answers attached. What the tool may do with it depends entirely on the platform, read live from the capability matrix rather than assumed.

**The moment** — Listening runs only when you ask.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *tier 1 platform* — send path exists, message goes out on per-message approval
- *tier 2/3 platform* — draft only, cued to you to send by hand regardless of approval

### Finding a real person behind a company name

**Set up** — company and role known, name and email are not

**Show** — Hiring manager first, recruiter-track staged separately and framed differently. Neither is ever asserted with certainty — the output is a confidence-scored hypothesis with its sources attached.

**The moment** — You approve each lookup.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *free cascade only* — public sources, self-hosted tools, then rotated free tiers — 325+ lookups a month at zero cost
- *paid tier reached* — asks, with the price, and only if a budget above zero was set

### Cold pitch to a company with no opening

**Set up** — reach out to X, or the prospecting cadence cron

**Show** — Every claim about their situation traces to a line in that company's own research record, and is framed as a hypothesis rather than an assertion.

**The moment** — You approve each research pass. Applications wait on you.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *role_fit* — "I could do X for you" — familiar, low friction, the default volume
- *role_creation* — asks them to accept a diagnosis of their own business first — deliberately a small minority of weekly volume

### LinkedIn stranger, connect then message

**Set up** — target identified on LinkedIn, no existing connection

**Show** — Two gates in sequence, and the second cannot fire until the platform prerequisite clears. A drafted message sits below sendable until the connection is actually accepted, no matter how ready it is.

**The moment** — Each request is shown with the note attached, then sent at human pace on your own logged-in session.

### Interview invite to prep brief

**Set up** — interview_request_at set, by email scan or by you

**Show** — Fires on the invite arriving, not on you remembering to ask. Where three likely questions have no story on file, the brief says exactly that rather than filling the space.

**The moment** — You confirm each scan. Slower, and outcomes get logged later than they happened.

### Live practice session

**Set up** — 'quiz me' / 'practise for Thursday'

**Show** — The study phase, separate from the build phase. Runs on the deck already built, and works by voice as readily as by text.

### Offer comparison and decision

**Set up** — an offer arrives

**Show** — The other end of the pipeline and the higher-stakes decision. Built as a workbook you can change the assumptions in, not as prose that argues for an answer.

## Act 5 — Direction and presence  ·  5 min

### Plan a path to a target role

**Set up** — what would it take to become a X

**Show** — Ends in one confirmed target, which then becomes what the whole discovery pipeline runs against. Two hops maximum; a third rests on a profile nobody can predict.

**The moment** — A suggestion to widen your search to adjacent titles is proposed and you accept or decline it.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *allow_comp_regression ask* — steps that pay less are shown with the reasoning
- *allow_comp_regression never* — silently removes most sector switches and management-track entries

### Build and publish a portfolio page

**Set up** — a role asks for a portfolio, or you want a link

**Show** — Six blocks, every one drawn from already-confirmed memory. Built locally first — going public is its own decision, made after you have looked at it.

**The moment** — The page is built locally and you open it before deciding whether it goes to a public address.

### LinkedIn profile audit and rewrite

**Set up** — audit my LinkedIn

**Show** — Every proposed line traces to the same confirmed material a resume draws on. Approval is per change, because your network sees the edit activity.

**The moment** — Each headline, summary or role change is proposed one at a time with the current text beside the new one.

## Act 6 — It gets better  ·  5 min

### Weekly self-improvement review

**Set up** — Monday morning

**Show** — Correlates your tactics against your real outcomes and proposes specific, evidence-backed edits — with the numbers behind each one. Two proposals a week, not eight, because a review that surfaces eight teaches you to approve all of them without reading.

**The moment** — "Values-alignment section shows no response-rate difference over 40 applications — recommend making it optional." You read the proposed edit and the numbers behind it before it takes effect.

**If they ask what happens otherwise**

- *rotation group released* — only this week's group of proposals surfaces, the rest wait with their detection date recorded
- *effect size grew* — a queued proposal is promoted to the next release regardless of its group

### Share an outcome milestone

**Set up** — response rate crosses 2x your stated baseline over 10+ applications

**Show** — Triggered on a personal multiple, not an absolute number, so it means something regardless of your field. Capped at one ask a fortnight and suppressed entirely after two you ignore.

**The moment** — After your sixth interview invite, a short post is drafted in your own writing style with the underlying numbers shown beside it. You edit or discard it.

### Pause and resume the search

**Set up** — pause for two weeks

**Show** — Discovery stops. Conversation does not, and the journal keeps running — a pause is about the searching, not about you disappearing.

**The moment** — Pausing and resuming are always your call, and a pause has a visible expiry.

## What not to demo

Fourteen flows are deliberately unmarked. Backup restore, partial install, template saving, profile monitoring, later interview rounds, InMail, skill composition. Each is real and useful and none of them earns floor time — a demo that covers everything communicates nothing.

**One exception worth reconsidering.** `FLOW-H4`, the partial install, is unmarked and is the single most reassuring thing in the package for a technical buyer: the system refuses to run half-configured and names the missing piece. If the audience is technical, trade a minute of Act 4 for it.

## The three beats that matter most

**The evidence gate refusing a claim.** Every competitor in this space demonstrates output volume. Almost none demonstrates their tool declining to say something. It is the most differentiating thirty seconds available, and it lands hardest immediately after Act 2, while the audience is still impressed by the speed and quietly wondering what the catch is.

**The submit block.** A submit-shaped action with no recorded approval, stopped by a check that reads the database rather than the conversation. Show the refusal, then show the reason in plain language.

**The weekly review.** Not that it proposes a change — that it proposes a change *with the numbers behind it* and then waits. Two proposals, not eight: a review that surfaces eight teaches people to approve all of them without reading, and that is worth saying out loud during the demo.

## Ground rules

- **Real data, one profile, throughout.** Switching profiles mid-demo reads as hiding something.

- **Never demo a send.** Show the approval message and stop. Sending a real message to a real stranger to prove a point is the one thing that would undermine the entire trust argument the demo is making.

- **Let one thing fail on purpose.** Act 3 exists for this. A demo where nothing goes wrong is a demo nobody believes.
