How this works

38 worked situations · 38 points where it asks you first · 22 settings. Every one shown both ways, so you can see what the choice actually costs.

Getting started

What happens the first time you run it.

First install, experienced candidate

a fresh install with nothing set up yet

Session one, run to completion: pair the approval channel, then Phase 0-4 of the career interview, then the handful of settings the pipeline cannot run without. Everything else is spread across the following fortnight.

First install, first-time entrant

ingestion returns empty or education-only

A genuinely different first session, not a lower bar on the same one. Interests and coursework carry the weight that work history carries elsewhere, and the match thresholds start lower because entry-level postings overstate their own requirements.

Paced advanced settings, sessions 2..N

any later session, paced by observed cadence

Spread over one to two weeks, ordered by dependency and payoff. Pitch catalog seeding is deliberately last and gets its own session.

Adding one job source

add this board / watch this company's careers page

Its own micro-flow, run any time, distinct from onboarding the person. The posted_at method matters more than the URL — it is what makes the speed metric honest.

Applying for jobs

The main loop, from a posting appearing to an application going out.

Scheduled discovery to staged application

discovery every 3h, sweep on its own cadence

The spine. A posting appears, is parsed, scored, researched, tailored, gated for evidence, and arrives as one message asking approve, edit or skip. Nothing between the posting appearing and that message needs you.

  • strict fidelity — unsupported claims stripped, gap written to open_gaps, nothing held for review
  • balanced fidelity — claims applied and tagged UNVERIFIED, held for review before the the approval message
  • stretch match — scores 50-70 staged and tagged STRETCH in the the approval message
  • parallel sweep — build phase delegated across postings, status flows building to staged to awaiting_approval

Applying to a posting you found yourself

pasted URL or job description

The same pipeline entered halfway. Skips discovery and the daily cap entirely — a posting you brought is never rationed.

Score a role without applying

how well do I match this

Read-only. Produces a score and the specific gaps behind it, and stops. Often the honest answer to "should I bother with this one."

Edit before approving

reply 'edit' to an the approval message

An edit re-enters the evidence gate rather than bypassing it, and the edit itself is logged — the learning loop needs to know which packages needed changing.

Skip or ignore a staged application

reply 'skip', or silence

Silence is never consent. The application sits, and it is counted as a decision you made rather than one the system made for you.

Answering a long application form

posting carries free-text questions

Answers are recomposed from confirmed stories. Where no story covers the question, the gap is surfaced rather than invented, and the question joins the bank for next time.

Reaching people directly

For roles that were never advertised.

Reply to a hiring post found on social

social listening scan

A post asking for candidates becomes a drafted response with the post it answers attached. What the tool may do with it depends entirely on the platform, read live from the capability matrix rather than assumed.

  • tier 1 platform — send path exists, message goes out on per-message approval
  • tier 2/3 platform — draft only, cued to you to send by hand regardless of approval
  • public reply — labelled REPLY, goes up under your name where the whole thread sees it

Cold pitch to a company with no opening

reach out to X, or the prospecting cadence on a schedule

Every claim about their situation traces to a line in that company's own research record, and is framed as a hypothesis rather than an assertion.

  • role_fit — "I could do X for you" — familiar, low friction, the default volume
  • role_creation — asks them to accept a diagnosis of their own business first — deliberately a small minority of weekly volume
  • wildcard entry used — WILDCARD tag carried through to the the approval message so approval is never given on autopilot

Finding a real person behind a company name

company and role known, name and email are not

Hiring manager first, recruiter-track staged separately and framed differently. Neither is ever asserted with certainty — the output is a confidence-scored hypothesis with its sources attached.

  • free cascade only — public sources, self-hosted tools, then rotated free tiers — 325+ lookups a month at zero cost
  • paid tier reached — asks, with the price, and only if a budget above zero was set

LinkedIn stranger, connect then message

target identified on LinkedIn, no existing connection

Two gates in sequence, and the second cannot fire until the platform prerequisite clears. A drafted message sits below sendable until the connection is actually accepted, no matter how ready it is.

InMail instead of connect-and-wait

target worth a metered credit

Buys skipping the wait, at the cost of a plan-gated credit. One per recipient — no follow-up without a reply. Unavailable until your subscription tier is set, which is the honest default rather than an error.

Seeding the pitch catalog

its own dedicated session, never folded into general setup

A genuinely creative pass, not a checkbox. Stretch entries get a visibly heavier confirmation than held ones — an explicit "you are telling me you can actually deliver this."

Interviews and offers

From the invite arriving to the decision.

Interview invite to prep brief

interview_request_at set, by email scan or by you

Fires on the invite arriving, not on you remembering to ask. Where three likely questions have no story on file, the brief says exactly that rather than filling the space.

Live practice session

'quiz me' / 'practise for Thursday'

The study phase, separate from the build phase. Runs on the deck already built, and works by voice as readily as by text.

Later round or a changed format

a later round's details arriving by email

A second round is a different interview, not the same one again — panel, format and depth all move.

Offer comparison and decision

an offer arrives

The other end of the pipeline and the higher-stakes decision. Built as a workbook you can change the assumptions in, not as prose that argues for an answer.

Working out what to aim at

When the question is which job, not which application.

Plan a path to a target role

what would it take to become a X

Ends in one confirmed target, which then becomes what the whole discovery pipeline runs against. Two hops maximum; a third rests on a profile nobody can predict.

  • allow_comp_regression ask — steps that pay less are shown with the reasoning
  • allow_comp_regression never — silently removes most sector switches and management-track entries
  • liquidity probe off — taxonomy signals only — a plan can point at a title with no live market

Build or refresh the interests profile

setup, or something interest-shaped mentioned in passing

Recording is free and unrestricted. Whether a sensitive entry ever appears in an outward document is a separate decision, asked at the moment of use, every time.

Career pulse journal check-in

journal cadence

Raw entries are stored immediately; anything that looks like a durable career fact goes through confirmation before it reaches memory. A quantified claim heard in a voice note is read back before it is written.

Profile monitor picks up a change

explicit-channel monitor cadence

Watches the profiles you named. Surfaces, never writes — every candidate fact goes through the same confirmation as one you mentioned yourself.

Your public presence

Portfolio, profile, and what you choose to share.

Build and publish a portfolio page

a role asks for a portfolio, or you want a link

Six blocks, every one drawn from already-confirmed memory. Built locally first — going public is its own decision, made after you have looked at it.

LinkedIn profile audit and rewrite

audit my LinkedIn

Every proposed line traces to the same confirmed material a resume draws on. Approval is per change, because your network sees the edit activity.

Save a named output template

always write cover letters for X like this

The one confirmed-write file that does not route through the memory skill, deliberately — a formatting preference is not a career fact.

Share an outcome milestone

response rate crosses 2x your stated baseline over 10+ applications

Triggered on a personal multiple, not an absolute number, so it means something regardless of your field. Capped at one ask a fortnight and suppressed entirely after two you ignore.

How it improves

What changes over time, and what you approve first.

Weekly self-improvement review

Monday morning

Correlates your tactics against your real outcomes and proposes specific, evidence-backed edits — with the numbers behind each one. Two proposals a week, not eight, because a review that surfaces eight teaches you to approve all of them without reading.

  • rotation group released — only this week's group of proposals surfaces, the rest wait with their detection date recorded
  • effect size grew — a queued proposal is promoted to the next release regardless of its group

Quarterly optimiser run

manual, quarterly, never scheduled

Deliberately manual and deliberately scoped to the three writing skills. The risk gate is excluded — an optimiser whose constraint checks cannot see content is the wrong tool to point at the one skill whose value is being conservative.

Ghost check and outcome nudge

daily

Scans for outcomes first so the daily ask is scoped to what has no email trace at all — a verbal offer from a call, or real silence.

Pause and resume the search

pause for two weeks

Discovery stops. Conversation does not, and the journal keeps running — a pause is about the searching, not about you disappearing.

Adding a new capability

can it also do X

Defaults to writing an addendum rather than rewriting a working file, so hand-tuned behaviour is never silently replaced.

When something goes wrong

The situations you will actually hit, and what happens in each.

The posting disappeared before approval

re-verification before the the approval message

Three signals, and the third catches most real cases: a 404, a page that now says closed, and a redirect to the board index where the fetch succeeds and only the content tells you. A pulled posting is information, not a decision — and it must not count as a non-reply, or every response rate you measure is understated.

The evidence gate refuses a claim

a tactic with no supporting line in memory

The gap goes to a worklist, never into memory as an invented fact. The memory interview picks it up on its next run, which is how a refused claim becomes a real story rather than a permanent blocker.

Submit blocked by the safety hook

a submit-shaped click with no approval recorded

The hook trusts the recorded decision, never the conversation. It fails closed on every branch it can see — and fails open, silently, on any tool name it was never told about, which is why the form is not opened at all until the toolset is confirmed watched.

Partial install

an incomplete install

The worst failure available here is not a crash. It is a skill that reads convincingly with no rules, no database and no profile behind it — which is why this is checked at first run of every session.

Restore from backup

data loss or a bad migration

States what will be overwritten and from when, before it runs. Backups themselves are unattended and need no gate; restores are not and do.

What you get asked, and what happens if you stop being asked

Every one of these is a point where the tool stops and waits for you. Each shows what happens either way.

Things that cannot be undone

A real thing happens to someone else. You can switch these off, but it takes a deliberate step and it expires.

Submitting a job application

On

A Product Manager role at Acme is filled in and ready. You get a message with the company, the role, a screenshot of the completed form and the list of claims made. Nothing is sent until you reply approve. If you say nothing, it waits.

Off

The same application is submitted the moment it is built — typically within a minute of the posting being found. You find out it went out when it appears in your sent list. If a claim was wrong, it has already reached the employer.

Sending a direct message

On

A hiring manager posts that they are hiring. A message is drafted using your pitch catalog and shown to you with the post it responds to. You approve or edit it.

Off

The message sends itself. On a platform where automated sending is against the rules, the account carrying that risk is yours, not the tool's.

Sending a cold email

On

A pitch to a company you have researched is drafted, with every claim about their situation traced to a line in the research record. You read it before it sends.

Off

It sends on the research pass's own schedule. A wrong guess about a company's problems is now something you asserted to them in writing.

Spending an InMail credit

On

You are told which of your monthly credits this would use and how many remain, before it goes. One InMail per recipient — there is no follow-up without a reply.

Off

Credits are spent as targets are found. A month's allowance can be gone in a day, on recipients you never saw.

Sending a connection request

On

Each request is shown with the note attached, then sent at human pace on your own logged-in session.

Off

Requests go out in batches. LinkedIn's pattern detection is looking for exactly this shape, and the account it acts against is yours.

Commenting publicly under your name

On

A reply to a public thread is drafted and marked as going up publicly, visible to everyone in that thread, before you approve it.

Off

Comments post themselves under your name where colleagues and future employers can read them.

Editing your live LinkedIn profile

On

Off

Your public profile is rewritten. Your network sees the edit activity, and so does your current employer.

Publishing your portfolio page

On

The page is built locally and you open it before deciding whether it goes to a public address.

Off

It publishes on build. Anything drawn from memory that you would not have put on a public page is now on one.

Off

Settings

Simple

Asked during setup. The tool cannot work without these.

Whether you have prior work history

Default

Asked before anything else. It routes the whole first session and pre-sets your match thresholds — 55/35 instead of 70/50 if you are starting out, because entry-level postings systematically overstate their own requirements.

Changed

Assumed experienced. Someone with no work history gets a search tuned for someone who has one, and sees almost nothing.

Your seniority level

Default

Postings are filtered to your band before anything else runs, so the pipeline spends its daily budget on roles you could take.

Changed

Nothing downstream can filter. Graduate roles and director roles arrive in the same queue.

Where you will work

Default

Remote, hybrid, onsite, countries and cities are all separate answers, so 'remote anywhere' and 'hybrid in Lagos only' filter differently.

Changed

Every posting passes the location filter, including ones you could never take.

Your minimum acceptable pay

Default

Postings below your floor are dropped before they cost you a review. 'No floor set yet' is a valid, working answer.

Changed

You review applications for roles that could never pay enough, and find out at offer stage.

How strictly claims must be evidenced

Default

strict — every claim cites a line in your resume, portfolio or story bank. No evidence means the tactic is dropped and the gap flagged to you.

Changed

embellish — unsupported claims still go out, logged for the audit trail but not held up for review. You may have to defend them in an interview with nothing on file.

How widely to search

Default

poll_only — only the sources you configured. Predictable, cheap, and misses anything not on those boards.

Changed

open_web — adds a broader web sweep on a slower cadence. More coverage, more noise, more cost per day.

Job titles to search for

Default

You confirm each variant. Titles you actually held are suggested automatically; adjacent titles the taxonomy proposes are shown with the evidence behind them.

Changed

Nothing to search for. Discovery returns nothing at all until at least one title exists.

Advanced

Sensible defaults already. Change them when you have a reason.

Whether you need visa sponsorship

Default

Roles that explicitly cannot sponsor are filtered out.

Changed

Unset by default, and nothing filters on it. You apply to roles that will reject you at the first screening question.

Industries to never show you

Default

Named industries never reach your queue.

Changed

Empty is the normal state. Everything is considered.

Companies to never apply to

Default

Your current employer, or anywhere you have decided against, never appears.

Changed

Empty is the normal state.

How many applications to prepare per day

Default

starter — 15 a day. Enough for one person's active search, and a number you can actually review.

Changed

max — 200 a day. Your own review throughput becomes the bottleneck, not the pipeline. Nothing sends itself at any tier.

Whether match thresholds may adjust themselves

Default

hybrid — the maths runs on schedule, each proposed change is staged, and it only takes effect once you approve it.

Changed

auto — thresholds move on their own, logged but not approved. Your bar drifts and you find out from the results.

Minimum match score to stage an application

Default

70 by default, 55 if you are starting out. Below it, nothing is staged.

Changed

Lower it and you review more, weaker matches. Raise it and a thin market produces an empty queue.

Stretch band floor

Default

50-70 is the stretch band: staged, and tagged STRETCH in the the approval message so you know it is a reach before you approve.

Changed

Disable stretch and you only ever see comfortable matches. Most step-up roles live in this band.

How far below your level to consider

Default

balanced — title-seniority and compensation deltas are weighed as two separate axes.

Changed

relaxed — roles well below your level reach the queue. Sometimes right in a bad market, expensive in attention otherwise.

Whether you are currently employed

Default

Urgency and volume adapt. Being between roles changes what a sensible daily target is.

Changed

unspecified — the pipeline runs at a neutral pace regardless of how urgent your search actually is.

How many career moves a plan may span

Default

Two hops. A third rests on a profile nobody can predict, and the re-plan rule would regenerate it anyway.

Changed

Longer plans read as forecasts. They look impressive and the later hops are fiction.

Whether a plan may include a pay cut

Default

ask — a step that pays less is shown to you with the reasoning rather than hidden.

Changed

never — silently removes most sector switches and management-track entries. A default that hides options is worse than one that asks.

Check live demand for a planned role

Default

A read-only census of live postings across your sources over 90 days. Queues nothing.

Changed

Falls back to taxonomy signals only. A plan can point at a title with no live market.

Monthly budget for paid contact lookups

Default

Zero. The free cascade covers 325+ lookups a month before anything could cost money.

Changed

Set a figure and paid lookups run automatically up to it, without asking each time.

Your LinkedIn subscription tier

Default

Sets your monthly InMail allowance so the tool knows what it is spending.

Changed

Unset, and InMail routing is simply unavailable — every stranger routes through connect-and-wait instead. Not an error state, the honest default.

Domains to skip in open-web search

Default

Aggregator sites that republish the same postings stop cluttering the queue.

Changed

Empty. Open-web mode surfaces duplicates from scraper sites.
